# TDS Made Simple: A Complete Guide for Luxury Homebuyers in India

Purchasing a luxury home is one of life's biggest milestones. A truly seamless homebuying experience also requires clarity on regulatory and financial responsibilities, with one of the most important considerations being Tax Deducted at Source (TDS).

## What is TDS and Why Does it Matter?
TDS is the tax the government collects upfront on high-value transactions. For property purchases, it ensures transparency and regulates large sums.

## The Law Governing TDS on Property Purchases
Governed by Section 194-IA of the Income Tax Act, 1961. Applies when purchasing immovable property valued above ₹50 lakh.

## When Does TDS Apply?
- All immovable property (flats, penthouses, villas, plots except agricultural)
- Value exceeds ₹50 lakh
- Resident Seller: TDS at 1% under Section 194-IA
- NRI Seller: TDS at 20% under Section 195

Example: ₹90 lakh flat → TDS = ₹90,000

## How to Calculate TDS
TDS = 1% × Total Sale Consideration (or Stamp Duty Value, whichever higher)
Includes car parking, club membership, PLC, maintenance deposits.

## Buyer's Responsibility
1. Deduct TDS at time of payment
2. Deposit via Form 26QB on TIN-NSDL portal
3. Complete within 30 days from end of deduction month
4. Issue Form 16B to seller

## Non-Compliance Penalties
- Interest on delayed deduction/payment
- Section 271H penalties
- Registration challenges

## TDS applies to both ready-to-move and under-construction properties
The deciding factor is sale value, not possession status.
